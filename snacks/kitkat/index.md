Crisp wafer fingers covered in milk chocolate, snapped apart one finger at a
time. Rowntree's of York launched it in 1935 as *Rowntree's Chocolate Crisp*;
it became Kit Kat two years later. Nestlé bought Rowntree's in 1988 and makes
it everywhere except the US, where Hershey makes it under licence.

Japan is its second home. The name sounds like *kitto katsu* ("you'll surely
win"), so it is given to students before exams, and Nestlé Japan has released
hundreds of regional and seasonal flavours.
