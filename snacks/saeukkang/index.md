Curved shrimp-flavoured wheat crackers, often called South Korea's first
mass-produced snack (Nongshim itself counts an earlier one). It is widely said
to be modelled on Calbee's Kappa Ebisen, though it isn't
made under licence. The name joins *saeu* (shrimp) with *-kkang*, from the
founder's young daughter mispronouncing the folk song *Arirang*, and the 1970s jingle "my hand keeps reaching out" is still
well known. Feeding them to seagulls from ferries is a Korean tradition.
